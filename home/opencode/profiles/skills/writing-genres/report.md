# Research report

**Purpose.** Tell a colleague or decision-maker what was found, how sure we are, and what remains open, in the time they have. The reader asks: what is the answer? Can I rely on it? What should happen next?

## Structure

1. **Answer first.** Three to five sentences: the answer to the question, the confidence, the main caveat, the recommended next step.
2. **Findings.** One subsection per finding, each opening with the finding as a sentence. Evidence level stated (from `EVIDENCE.md`), with the supporting result or figure.
3. **What was ruled out.** Formulations or approaches that failed, and why. This saves the next person weeks.
4. **Open questions and risks**, ranked by how much they could change the answer.
5. **Method,** briefly, with pointers to the research files for detail.

## Tone and level of detail

- Skimmable: headings that are sentences, a table of findings with evidence levels, short paragraphs. Lists are fine for parallel items.
- Mathematical detail minimal; formulas only where the result is a formula.
- Confidence stated once per finding, using the evidence levels, not spread through the prose as hedges.
- Negative results stated as plainly as positive ones.
