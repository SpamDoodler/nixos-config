# Research procedure: files and phases

## Files you own

- `research/FRAMING.md`: what the goal means operationally (from `r-strategist`, edited by you).
- `research/PLAN.md`: success criteria, formulations being pursued, kill criteria, budget. Frozen after phase 2; changes go in `decisions.md` with a reason.
- `research/CLAIMS.md`: every claim the final answer may use, its status and evidence level. The report may only state claims from here.
- `research/STATUS.md`: short progress block per round (see goal mode), newest last.
- `research/decisions.md`, `research/negative-results.md`: append-only. Check `negative-results.md` before creating any task.
- `research/LEDGER.md`: generated by `ledger`. Never edit by hand.
- `research/tasks/<id>/card.md`: one per task.
- `research/report/REPORT.md`: the final answer.

## Phase 0: framing (do not skip, even for concrete-looking goals)

Goals are often abstract: "work out the relation between A and B". That is a legitimate goal, but it is not yet a task anyone can execute. Turning it into concrete questions is the highest-leverage decision in the project, and the most common source of solving the wrong problem.

1. Read the goal and the workspace's obvious entry points. Launch `r-recon-ws` and `r-recon-lit` in parallel; they are cheap.
2. Launch `r-strategist` in `frame` mode with the goal, the recon summaries, and tell it to use `framing-template.md` from the `research-planning` skill. It returns `research/FRAMING.md`: precise meanings of the terms (with alternatives where ambiguous), the candidate formulations (for "relation between A and B": identity, limit or special case, map or duality, bound, controlled approximation, common origin, empirical correlation, no relation or an obstruction), a cheap discriminating test for each, the form a satisfying answer would take, and a glossary.
3. Skip the strategist only when the goal is already a concrete, checkable question and recon found nothing that changes it. Record that decision.
4. Write `research/CONVENTIONS.md`: notation, units, sign and normalisation conventions, at most 40 lines. If the user's config lists it under `instructions` (see the guide), every subagent sees it automatically; otherwise list it as an input on every card.

## Phase 1: plan

Write `research/PLAN.md` from the template. Pursue a **portfolio**: 2 to 4 formulations in parallel at spike depth (cheap tasks that test feasibility and discriminate between formulations) before committing serious effort to one. A negative result ("A and B are not related in sense X, because Y") is a valid outcome; say so in the success criteria.

Interactive session: ask the user to confirm the framing and plan once (question tool), then freeze. Under /goal: do not wait. Freeze the plan yourself, list your interpretation and open assumptions at the top of `STATUS.md` so the user can correct you, and proceed.

## Phase 2: organise the program for parallelism

How the work is cut decides how much runs in parallel, and therefore how fast and how cheaply the goal is reached. Read `decomposition.md` in the `research-planning` skill before building the first graph.

1. **Contracts first.** Every shared decision (notation, normalisation, definitions, data formats, the exact statement two tasks must agree on) becomes an early contract task. Later tasks depend on the contract, not on each other.
2. **Independent tasks.** A task owns its output folder, reads only finished inputs, and can be checked on its own. If two tasks must talk, there is a missing contract.
3. **Wide, not deep.** Prefer many small tasks over long chains: split by case, parameter range, regime, or method; run cross-checks, literature and numerics beside the main line, not after it.
4. **Portfolio spikes.** Each formulation in `PLAN.md` gets cheap spike tasks in parallel. No formulation gets deep work before it has beaten its alternatives on a spike.
5. **Measure the shape.** Register the tasks with `ledger add`, write each card, then call `ledger stats`. If parallelism is below the target (`tiers.json`, default 2.5) with eight or more tasks, restructure, or record why the problem is inherently serial with `ledger gate kind=structure note=...`. Look at the bottleneck tasks it lists: give them priority, a higher tier, or split them.

Per task: a card from the template with a context pack (goal in one line, the formulation it serves, the glossary); acceptance criteria a checker can test without asking; priority (P1 = the conclusion depends on it; few tasks deserve it); verification level (V1 default, V2 if a claim rests on it, V3 for P1, V4 for headline claims). For the benchmark, also:

- **type**, one of `lookup`, `literature`, `derivation`, `numerics`, `code`, `analysis`, `explore`, `other` (keep to these so the data accumulates);
- **tags** for what the task demands, from a small vocabulary: `symbolic`, `search`, `long-context`, `coding`, `conceptual`, `multi-step`, `numerical-care`. The report shows pass rates per tag and model, which is how you learn what suits the small models;
- **difficulty** (best guess, 1-5, anchored with `bench similar`) and, whenever you are not sure, a **difficulty range** such as `"2-4"`. The range is your confidence: a wide range says you do not know, and the ledger then probes cheaper models. The report checks whether wide ranges really escalate more often; if they do not, your ranges carry no information and you should tighten them.

## Phase 3: pre-mortem

Launch `r-skeptic` in `premortem` mode with only `FRAMING.md`, `PLAN.md`, `LEDGER.md` and the `ledger stats` output. Act on it, then `ledger gate kind=premortem note=<what you changed>`. The ledger flags this as required until it is recorded.

## Phase 4: dispatch and check (the loop)

Each round:

1. **Process reports** that arrived:
   - worker `DONE` or `PARTIAL` -> `ledger check id=...` and launch every checker it names: the regular one and, sometimes, a cross-check one model down or up (benchmark data). Each writes its own verdict file. For V3 tasks, wait until both the worker and the replica reported.
   - all verdicts in -> `ledger verdict` with the regular verdict and `check_verdicts` for the cross-checks. If they disagree, the ledger says what to do: launch an adjudicator (astra for terra vs sol; sol for a sampled luna disagreement) or, for sol vs astra, send each checker the other's verdict as a follow-up and record both final verdicts. Then `ledger adjudicate`. If sol and astra still disagree, the task is blocked and goes to the user as a question.
   - checker verdict -> `ledger verdict` with the checker's verdict, the checker's tier, `fixable_local` if the checker says the fix is local, and both session ids. Then do exactly what it returns: mark claims, send a repair follow-up to the same worker session, append the failure summary and re-dispatch, or re-scope.
   - `NEEDS_SPLIT` -> add the proposed tasks (deduplicate against the ledger and `negative-results.md`), then `ledger update status=split`.
   - `BLOCKED` -> fix the card or its inputs, or drop the task with a note.
   - `WRONG_QUESTION` -> zoom-out gate before anything else.
   - V4 pass -> run `r-redteam` and record `ledger gate kind=redteam id=... verdict=pass|fail note=...` before citing the result.
   - probe worker finishes -> `ledger probe_finished id=... probe=<tier>` immediately to free its slot. After the main result is verified (including red-team for V4), run `ledger check id=... probe=<tier>` and `ledger verdict id=... probe=<tier>` with distinct worker and Terra checker session IDs. Use `ledger probe_ungraded id=... probe=<tier> note=...` for missing inputs, incomparable runs, an aborted run, no cleared reference, or a Terra abstention. Do not use Sol or Astra to settle a benchmark-only grade.
2. **Fill the slots.** `ledger ready`, then `ledger dispatch` for the best ready tasks until the slots are full. Launch exactly the subagents each dispatch names (worker, replica, probes) in the same turn, with the one-line prompt it gives. Probes have their own slots and never block main work.
3. **End the round** with `ledger status` using `focus` (the 1–3 main problems), `active_routes` (approaches and what each tests), `changed` (a meaningful finding or decision), `blockers` (what is awaited and what would unblock it), and `next`. Omit empty sections; the ledger supplies a short in-flight task list. Summarize the same update to the user in plain language. After the first dispatch or a material change, surface the update before waiting for workers; do not add a separate round just to send status. Distinguish promising findings from verified results and pending gates.

**Checks go one model up** (the ledger enforces it): luna by `r-verifier-t` (terra); terra by `r-verifier-s` (sol); sol by `r-verifier-s` in a fresh session, or by `r-verifier-a` (astra) for priority-1 work; astra by `r-verifier-s`. A checker of the same model tends to share the worker's blind spots.

**First tier.** Dispatch without a tier uses the routed one, which already prices money and time. Overriding needs a note. Difficulty 4-5 never starts below sol, priority 1 never at luna.

**Failures.** One repair per attempt by follow-up to the same worker session, if the checker says the fix is local. Otherwise the next dispatch goes one tier up with the checker's failure summary (100 words at most) appended to the card. After sol: astra if priority 1, otherwise re-scope, split or drop. Two failures of the same task type at the same tier: re-grade that type.

**Probes (automatic).** For every first difficulty-5 dispatch, launch Luna and Terra in the same batch as the main run, before reading any result. If two probe slots aren't free, leave that task queued and free slots from completed workers; don't start the main run alone or consume the reserved openings with optional probes. Do not retroactively call post-result attempts blind. In balanced/max mode Luna runs broadly and Terra is sampled up front on eligible difficulty-3/4 starts, independent of the eventual verdict.

Prepare a self-contained card or stage just the necessary answer-free inputs in `research/tasks/<id>/probe-inputs/` before dispatch, with exact paths on the card; all tiers should use the same inputs and acceptance criteria. The blind workers can read staged packets, not search the repository. Do not change the frozen inputs during the run. If a task inherently needs search, shell or unstaged dependencies, or inputs change, mark the comparison ungraded rather than treating the constrained attempt as a model failure. For long-context searches, record corpus size and search scope before assigning difficulty.

**Multi-route attempts (opt-in).** For a hard or open task, several cheaper workers can attack the same goal by distinctly different routes: `ledger dispatch id=... routes=3 tier=terra route_notes=[...]`. First write one card per route under `routes/r1..rN/card.md`: the same goal and acceptance criteria as the main card, but a different approach and a differently framed prompt (for example: perturbative, numerical scan then fit, analogy to a known system; or "prove it", "find a counterexample", "find it in the literature"). The task passes if any route passes the check. Use it when the approach is unclear, when sol has failed and the task is not P1, or to explore an open question cheaply. Not by default: it multiplies slots.

**Later contradictions.** When a replication, a downstream task or the integration check contradicts an earlier verified result, `bench label task_id=... label=bad source=...`. This is how the benchmark measures checker reliability.

## Phase 5: gates

The ledger marks gates as required when they are due: a zoom-out after every few verified tasks (`gate_every`), after any `WRONG_QUESTION`, and when a branch has spent a quarter of its budget without a verified claim (judge that one yourself). Launch `r-skeptic` in `zoomout` mode with `FRAMING.md`, `PLAN.md`, `LEDGER.md`, `CLAIMS.md`, `decisions.md`, `negative-results.md` and `ledger stats`, never worker output. Record each gate with `ledger gate`.

If the skeptic returns `wrong-problem` or `refocus` on a P1 branch, launch `r-strategist` in `reframe` mode and record `ledger gate kind=reframe`.

## Phase 6: integration verification

Individually verified pieces can still be inconsistent with each other (conventions, signs, regimes). Before the report:

1. `r-verifier-s` for consistency across `CLAIMS.md` and the task results it cites.
2. `r-verifier-t` for independent sanity checks on each headline claim (dimensions, limits, special cases, numerics), at physics-level rigor unless `PLAN.md` says otherwise.
3. For any disagreement, downgrade the claim and fix or flag it. Label the affected outcomes in `bench` with source `integration`.
4. `ledger gate kind=integration note=...`.

## Phase 7: synthesis and completion

Write `REPORT.md`: the answer in the form promised in `FRAMING.md`; each claim with its evidence level and the tasks behind it; the formulations that were ruled out and why; open assumptions; what would change the conclusion. Then `r-strategist` in `final` mode, and `ledger gate kind=final note=...`.
